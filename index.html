02

THE INVESTIGATION

I didn't want the project to end with a survey.

Recent flooding made me think about how engineers can help protect people in Nepal. I designed an anonymous online survey and collected 31 responses, asking students about engineering, infrastructure, disaster impacts, and what they thought engineers should prioritize.

The result wasn't a national study or a claim about everyone. It was a small, exploratory look at what appeared in this group — and a starting point for asking a more practical question:

What can I actually build from what I learned?

04 · FROM RESEARCH → DESIGN

One result kept pulling me toward a prototype.

Twelve respondents selected early-warning systems and technology as an important area for engineers. So I built a simple student-level flood early-warning concept: measure → compare with a threshold → trigger a warning.

It is a simulation, not a field-ready emergency system.

ABOUT THE RESEARCHER

Presika Thapa

Student Researcher · Future Civil Engineer · Nepal

I built this project to explore a question that felt close to home: how can engineering help communities become better prepared for hazards?

This is a student-led exploratory project. The goal is not to present a finished engineering solution, but to show the process of moving from an observed problem, to evidence, to a question, and finally to a small design that can be tested.
